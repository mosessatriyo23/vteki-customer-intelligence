## Contract to preserve and decisions still needed

Bab 2.2 defines gross-revenue CLV as historical spend over 12 months plus projected revenue over the next 12 months, with the illustrative formula `Historical Spend (12m) + Avg Txn Value × Freq/Mo × 12 × Retention_Factor`. It gives tiers and flags customers with fewer than two transactions as `INSUFFICIENT_HISTORY` (excluded from tier filtering). This proposal does **not** set a retention value, change tier boundaries, turn gross revenue into profit, or infer that a development `clv90` label measures 12-month CLV. The requirement does not define a retention estimator, the gross-revenue event/refund convention, or calendar months versus a rolling-day window. Obtain Christ's written decision on those points, the target population and the meaning of retention before implementing a business CLV calculation. The current development calculator takes a caller-supplied factor; it does not authorize a default.

Treat retention as the expected share of *baseline future transaction opportunity* that remains observable/eligible over the 12-month projection, conditional on information available at the scoring cutoff. A survival curve `S(t | X_asof)` can yield an **effective factor** `R = Σ_m w_m S(m | X_asof) / Σ_m w_m`, where `w_m` is pre-cutoff expected gross-revenue opportunity for future month `m`. If and only if monthly opportunity is assumed constant, equal month weights reduce this to the mean of monthly survival probabilities; in that case `Avg Txn Value × Freq/Mo × 12 × R` is the proposed projection. Weights must be estimated from training history only and documented, never chosen using holdout outcomes. This is an interpretation for review, not a declaration that churn causes all future revenue to stop: reactivation, membership revenue and episodic purchases can violate it. Validate projected **gross revenue** directly as well as any survival diagnostic. Do not substitute `1 - churn90` for a 12-month factor or use a 90-day score's 30-day validity to extend an estimate through 12 months.

## Candidate approaches (compare, do not select yet)

| Approach | Estimand and advantages | Key limitations / checks |
|---|---|---|
| Matured-cohort empirical retention | At each as-of date, form eligible customers and observe the subsequent 12-month purchase/visit/renewal continuity only for cohorts with a fully observable horizon. Estimate month-specific retained fractions in predeclared historical strata, with shrinkage/fallback learned from training cohorts. Transparent reference, few modeling assumptions. | Selection toward older cohorts and changing customer mix; immature cohorts must not be silently treated as churn. Sparse strata need uncertainty and an explicit unavailable/fallback status, not an invented factor. |
| Time-to-inactivity survival | Define an approved event of loss of activity and risk origin at the cutoff; estimate month-level survival with right-censoring at last reliably observed time. Kaplan–Meier or a predeclared discrete-time hazard are candidates; conditional model may use only as-of covariates. | Requires agreed inactivity/renewal/visit event semantics and observation/availability history. Administrative censoring may be informative; delayed entry, intermittent purchasing and reactivation may require a multi-state/recurrent-event approach rather than absorbing churn. Test proportional-hazard assumptions if using such a model. |
| Direct revenue projection comparator | Estimate future 12-month gross revenue (with eligible mature labels or a justified censor-aware approach) without forcing activity survival to proxy revenue. | Not a retention factor by itself; use to check whether a factor-based projection is fit for purpose. Do not retrofit a factor from test labels. |

For either retention approach, define the eligible customer universe, cohort origin, inclusion/exclusion, exposure time, event and renewal logic **before** inspection. Lifecycle `CHURNED` (>365-day inactivity plus membership expiry in Bab 1) is not automatically a survival event. Bab 2.1 simultaneously describes 180 days of no transaction/visit/renewal and a 90-day prediction horizon; Christ must resolve that conflict and its relationship, if any, to the CLV retention estimand. Membership renewal is revenue and may keep a customer active; do not mark a renewal as churn. Decide explicitly whether reactivation resets risk, is a transition, or is included in a revenue-only reference; document treatment of death of observation, identity merges, refunds, late status corrections, and customers entering mid-history.

## Censoring, point-in-time, and uncertainty controls

- A label at cutoff `t` is mature only when the **entire** agreed 12-month future outcome window has elapsed by the data availability boundary, including permitted settlement lag. Otherwise mark incomplete and exclude from ordinary full-horizon regression metrics; for survival, right-censor at the last *known observable* instant, not at the last transaction. Never code missing follow-up as zero revenue or a churn event. Record exposure and censor reason; test informative-censor sensitivity and coverage by cohort.
- Construct historical spend, value, frequency, membership state, visits and identity using event time **and** what the system knew at `t` (`available_at`/`known_at`, versioned corrections and identity-as-of). An event timestamp before `t` but arriving afterward is unavailable at `t`. Refund and transaction final-state projections need historical versions. The current retrospective resolver and event-time-only checks do not satisfy this condition; Gate C must establish the feed and replay evidence first.
- Keep outcome generation separate from features. Do not read generator ground truth, future renewals, future identity links, full-window totals, post-cutoff status or post-cutoff campaign decisions as predictors. Freeze customer/cohort membership at cutoff; retain audit keys and source lineage. Historical and projected gross amounts must use the same Christ-approved revenue convention without double-counting overlapping windows.
- Report confidence intervals (e.g., customer-clustered bootstrap with time-aware resampling), cohort counts, risk-set and censor counts by month, effective sample sizes and coverage; distinguish sampling uncertainty from business-definition and missing-feed uncertainty. Do not manufacture a precise customer-level factor for unsupported strata. Compare sensitivity to reasonable predeclared censor/event assumptions and mark outputs unavailable when assumptions cannot be defended.
